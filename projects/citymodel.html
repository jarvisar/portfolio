City Model

Web app | 2026

Open City Model Examples

The Chicago Loop in the 3D view after generating.

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors and Overture Maps, elevation from Mapterhorn, and public LiDAR surveys.

About

City Model turns any area of the map into a multicolor 3D printable model, sized for FDM printing with a 0.4 mm nozzle. It builds terrain, water, parks, roads, buildings, and trees from Overture Maps and public elevation data, and exports a Bambu Studio project with one filament per color.

Where a public LiDAR survey covers the area, buildings can be measured from it, or the whole model can be built from the survey alone as one solid. The app reads surveys from more than 50 sources in North America, Europe, South America, and Japan. Models can be rectangles, circles, or hexagons, can include GPX routes and racetrack laps in their own color, and can be colored by height or with one of sixteen themes. Models larger than the print bed are split into plates. An editor in the 3D view changes buildings, roads, and land cover, and adds text and pins.

Background

City Model is the web version of my 3D Map Creator Blender add-on, with the same generation rules ported to TypeScript, so there is nothing to install. The add-on is written in Python, with a separate Python environment that downloads Overture Maps data and reads LiDAR with laspy, pyproj, and shapely. It came out of the Jarvizar miniature maps I design by hand.

How It Works

  • Everything runs in the browser. The model is generated in a Web Worker and never reaches a server.
  • Overture Maps data is read directly from its GeoParquet files on S3 with HTTP range requests. The release's STAC index and each file's footer narrow it down to the row groups that cover the area, and geometry is only downloaded for the rows inside it.
  • Elevation comes from Mapterhorn's terrain tiles (Copernicus GLO-30 and national models). Every layer is projected from WGS84 into one local east-north-up frame and samples the same height grid.
  • LiDAR comes from more than 50 publishers, including USGS 3DEP, NOAA Digital Coast, NRCan, and IGN, as EPT, COPC, Esri I3S, or LAS and LAZ files. Point clouds are decoded with laz-rs in a pool of Web Workers and reprojected with proj4js. LiDAR only models use grids of up to 64 million cells. Missing coverage and obvious errors are reported to the publishers.
  • Every part is a closed solid, so slicers don't have to repair it.
  • Tests run in Vitest, with Playwright scripts for the app and a fuzzer for the editor. Turning a feature off must leave the exported files byte-for-byte the same.

Details

  • Search for a place, pick a preset, or drag the box on the map, then generate the model and check it in the 3D view before downloading.
  • Exports Bambu Studio and PrusaSlicer projects, 3MF with colors, STL, and glTF for rendering in Blender.
  • Undo and redo for area and settings changes, a searchable settings sidebar, and share links.
  • Installable as a web app.

Accounts & Tiers

Every tool works without an account, and the Free tier downloads models up to 256 mm with any change made in the editor. Maker, Pro, and Commercial add routes, text, drawn shapes, multi-plate export, LiDAR outside the free areas, and a license to sell prints, as a one-time purchase with lifetime access. They launch through a MakerWorld crowdfunding campaign that runs from November 10 to December 10, 2026.

The account API is a Cloudflare Worker with a D1 database. It stores only an email address, the sign-in method, and the tier, since models are built in the browser and never reach a server. Sign-in is a code sent by email or Google, with no passwords, and backers redeem the code from their pledge once to have their tier in every browser. A separate admin page behind Cloudflare Access handles accounts, the backer list, and redeem codes, and shows error reports and how many models are generated and downloaded.
